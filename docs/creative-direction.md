# AUREA DENTAL — Creative Direction

**Homepage visual direction · v1**
Markets: UAE · Saudi Arabia · United States · France — Languages: EN · AR · FR

Companion moodboard: `aurea-moodboard.png` (project assets).

---

## 1. Brand essence

**Aurea** — Latin for *golden*. Not gold as ornament; gold as standard.

The brand promise in one line:

> **The calm certainty of being in the best possible hands.**

Aurea Dental is not a clinic that looks like a spa, and not a hospital that
bought nice furniture. It is a third thing: **precision medicine delivered
with the grace of a great hotel.** Every visual decision on the homepage
must pass a single test — *does this make a discerning person feel both
safer and more drawn in?* Trust first, desire immediately after, in the
same breath.

### The five reference feelings, translated

| Reference | What we take | What we leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Apple** | Reduction. One idea per screen. Product (the smile, the clinic, the craft) photographed like a hero object. | Coolness of tone — Apple is neutral; we are warm. |
| **Linear** | Discipline. Hairline borders, perfect rhythm, nothing decorative that isn't doing work. | Darkness as default — our world is daylight. |
| **Porsche** | Engineering as luxury. Specifications presented with pride. The confidence to show the machine. | Aggression, speed, adrenaline. |
| **Aman Hotels** | Serenity. Stone, light, silence, arrival. The sense that everything has been anticipated. | Remoteness — Aman whispers from far away; we must also be reachable. |
| **Luxury private healthcare** | Credentials without bragging. Discretion. The feeling that your case is *known*, not processed. | Institutional beige, stock-photo doctors, clip-art crosses. |

### Brand adjectives (use these to judge every decision)

**Calm · Precise · Warm · Assured · Quiet · Golden**

And the anti-adjectives — if a design choice reads as any of these, kill it:
*clinical-cold, salesy, busy, trendy, cute, "medical-blue".*

## 2. Art direction

### The core concept: **"Porcelain & Gold"**

The entire homepage is built from two materials and one light source.

- **Porcelain** — warm whites, ivory, greige. The color of a flawless
  restoration, of Aman travertine, of a gallery wall. This is the *space*
  of the site: backgrounds, surfaces, air.
- **Gold** — champagne, not yellow; brushed, not shiny. This is the
  *signature*: it appears rarely, deliberately, and always means "this is
  the moment that matters" — the single call to action, a focus ring, a
  fine rule under a headline.
- **One light source** — soft, directional, morning daylight. Every
  photograph, every shadow in the UI, every gradient behaves as if lit
  from the same warm window. This single rule is what will make the page
  feel like one crafted object instead of assembled parts.

### Composition principles

1. **Silence is the luxury.** Whitespace is not empty; it is the most
   expensive material on the page. Minimum 60% of any viewport is
   breathing room. When in doubt, remove.
2. **One protagonist per viewport.** Each scroll-stop has exactly one
   hero: a face, a claim, a number, a door. Never two competing focal
   points.
3. **The arch.** A recurring, almost subliminal geometric motif: the soft
   arch — of a tooth crown, of an Aman doorway, of Arabic architecture.
   Used in image masks, in the curve of large radii, in section
   transitions. It is the brand's secret handshake; never literal, never
   a tooth icon.
4. **Asymmetric calm.** Editorial layouts — generous off-center
   compositions on a strict grid — not centered "template" symmetry.
   Symmetry is reserved for moments of authority (credentials, the CTA).
5. **Bilingual dignity.** Arabic is not a translation; it is a first-class
   art direction case. Layouts must be composed to be mirrored (RTL), and
   Arabic typography must look *designed*, never "swapped in".

## 3. Moodboard

Nine tiles, one story (see `aurea-moodboard.png`):

1. **Porcelain macro** — the product truth: flawless ceramic in raking
   warm light. This texture language appears in hero backgrounds.
2. **Brushed champagne gold** — the signature material. Matte, machined,
   Porsche-precise. Never gradient-gold, never bling.
3. **The clinic as an Aman lobby** — travertine, boucle, an olive tree,
   diffused sun. Architecture photography sells trust before any word.
4. **The serif specimen** — editorial confidence. Type *is* the brand
   voice; a headline should feel like the opening line of a monograph.
5. **The marble arch** — the recurring motif: a tooth silhouette abstracted
   into sculpture. Suggestion, never illustration.
6. **The genuine smile** — warm, editorial, lit like a beauty campaign but
   real. The emotional payoff of the entire brand.
7. **Gold geometric linework** — a restrained nod to Arabic geometry for
   the Gulf markets: hairline, mathematical, quiet.
8. **Water on glass** — freshness, hygiene, and the digital material of
   our UI: frosted glass surfaces over photography.
9. **The dark tile** — ink charcoal with a small gold wordmark: the
   evening-mode of the brand, used for moments of gravitas (the final CTA,
   the footer world).

## 4. Visual language

### Space & grid

- 12-column editorial grid, wide outer gutters that grow with the
  viewport. Content rarely spans more than 8 columns; text columns cap at
  ~65 characters.
- Vertical rhythm is *architectural*: big, consistent section spacing that
  makes scrolling feel like walking through rooms, each with its own
  purpose and its own pause before the next.
- Full-bleed is earned, not default: photography may go edge-to-edge;
  text never does.

### Materials (the UI's physical vocabulary)

- **Porcelain surfaces** — warm-white cards with hairline borders, almost
  flush with the background. Elevation is whispered (soft, warm-tinted
  shadows), never dropped like a sticker.
- **Frosted glass** — translucent, blurred surfaces reserved for elements
  that float over photography (navigation when it arrives, overlay
  panels). Glass says "modern precision" against warm stone.
- **Brushed gold** — solid champagne fills for the single most important
  action, and hairline gold rules as punctuation. Gold is never a large
  surface, never a background wash.
- **Ink** — near-black charcoal panels for the page's low, resonant
  notes: a statement of philosophy, the closing invitation.

### Line & detail

- Hairlines everywhere (1px, low-contrast) — the Linear discipline.
- Corners are soft and generous (the arch motif at micro scale), but
  never bubbly; radii are confident, not playful.
- Numbers matter: statistics, years, certifications set in refined
  figures with letterspaced small-cap labels — the Porsche spec-sheet
  instinct, aestheticized.

## 5. Color usage

The palette already lives in the design system (warm neutral scale +
champagne gold + muted status hues). The creative rules for *using* it:

### The 90 / 9 / 1 rule

- **~90% porcelain neutrals** — ivory backgrounds, warm-white surfaces,
  greige borders, ink text. The page *is* this family.
- **~9% ink** — deep charcoal: headline text, one or two dark "gravitas"
  sections, the footer world.
- **~1% gold** — and that 1% is why it works. One primary CTA per
  viewport, focus states, a hairline rule, a small wordmark, a dot of
  status. If gold appears twice in one glance, one of them is wrong.

### Rules of engagement

- Gold **never** sits on gold; gold text only on ink or porcelain.
- No pure white (`#FFF` feels like paper in fluorescent light); our
  lightest tone is warm.
- No blue. Medical-blue is the uniform of everything we are not. Trust is
  carried by warmth, typography, and evidence — not by the color of a
  hospital corridor.
- Status colors (success/warning/error) exist for UI honesty
  (forms, availability) and stay muted — they must feel like part of the
  same dye-lot, never alarm-bright.
- Dark sections are *ink*, not black — and inside them, gold gains
  slightly more presence (the evening register of the brand).

## 6. Typography philosophy

**The voice is a duet: an editorial serif that speaks, and a precision
sans that works.**

- **Display serif — Playfair Display** (already configured): headlines,
  statements, the brand's spoken voice. Set large, tight-tracked,
  unafraid. A Playfair headline over generous whitespace *is* the luxury —
  it does half the art direction on its own. Italic is permitted for
  single emphasized words within a headline ("the *quiet* standard"), a
  fashion-editorial gesture used at most once per page.
- **Working sans — Inter**: everything that must be effortless — body
  copy, UI, forms, captions, data. Inter disappears; that is its job.
- **Arabic duet — Amiri + IBM Plex Sans Arabic** (already configured):
  Amiri gives Arabic headlines genuine calligraphic dignity (never a
  geometric sans pretending to be neutral); Plex Sans Arabic carries UI
  and body with modern clarity. Arabic display type may sit slightly
  larger than its Latin equivalent — Arabic letterforms breathe lower and
  deserve the space.

### Hierarchy behavior

- Enormous jumps between levels — display type is *very* large, body is
  modest, and there is little in between. The gap itself communicates
  confidence (Apple's oldest trick).
- Captions and labels: small, letterspaced, medium weight — the
  spec-sheet register (measurements, credentials, locations).
- Never more than two type families visible per language. Never bold
  entire paragraphs. Never center long text.

## 7. Photography style

Photography is the credibility engine of this homepage. Three registers,
one light:

### A. Architecture (trust through place)

Aman-register interiors: stone, wood, linen, diffused morning sun, long
still compositions, no people or one out-of-focus figure. Wide, calm,
slightly low contrast. This says *we invested in a place worth your
presence* before a single claim is made.

### B. Portraiture (trust through people)

- **Patients:** editorial beauty photography — warm directional daylight,
  genuine micro-expressions, skin with texture (retouching removes
  blemishes, never pores). Casting reflects all four markets naturally —
  Emirati, Saudi, American, French faces — with elegance, without
  tokenism. Wardrobe: quiet luxury neutrals; no lab coats on patients,
  obviously — but also no evening gowns. Real, elevated.
- **Clinicians:** photographed like Porsche engineers — composed,
  confident, in impeccable but understated attire; hands and instruments
  in macro as craft objects. Never the arms-crossed stock-photo dentist,
  never looking up from a fake chart.

### C. Craft macro (desire through detail)

Extreme close-ups: porcelain surfaces, a polished instrument catching
light, water on glass, the curve of a ceramic crown like sculpture. These
are the "product shots" — the Apple hero-object treatment applied to
dentistry's materials.

### The non-negotiables

- One light: warm, directional, morning. No mixed color temperatures
  across the page.
- Grade: gentle warm highlights, soft blacks, muted saturation.
  No HDR, no teal-orange, no filters.
- **Banned forever:** gloved hands making heart shapes, mirror-and-drill
  close-ups in cold light, terrified-to-delighted before/afters as hero
  imagery, watermarked stock, fluorescent-lit interiors, veneer-white
  teeth whiter than the background.

## 8. Icon style

- **Hairline geometry** — 1.5px stroke, rounded caps, built on the same
  grid, monochrome ink (gold only when the icon *is* the accent moment).
- Abstract over literal: an arch, not a molar; a calendar mark, not a
  cartoon tooth with a face. Ideally the icon set feels closer to a
  luxury watch manual than to a medical app.
- Icons are furniture, not decoration: they appear only where they add
  scanning speed (contact, locations, credentials) — never as filler
  illustrations beside every paragraph.
- Must read perfectly at small sizes in both LTR and RTL contexts
  (directional icons mirror; symmetrical forms preferred).

## 9. Motion direction

**The motion brand is: *unhurried certainty*.** Nothing pops, nothing
bounces, nothing loops for attention. Things *settle*, the way a heavy
door closes in a quiet building.

- **Reveals:** content fades and rises a short distance on a long
  luxurious ease-out (the `luxury` curve already in the system) —
  deliberate, almost slow. If a transition feels 20% too slow in a review,
  it is probably right.
- **Choreography:** elements of a section arrive as a family with subtle
  stagger — never one-by-one theatrical entrances.
- **Interaction:** buttons and cards respond with quick, physical spring
  feedback (press → slight settle). Interactive motion is *fast*;
  narrative motion is *slow*. That contrast is the whole system.
- **The one indulgence:** a single slow, continuous hero moment — light
  moving across porcelain, a barely-perceptible parallax — one heartbeat
  per page, no more.
- **Scroll behavior:** the page never hijacks scroll. Parallax, if used,
  is measured in millimeters.
- **Respect for stillness:** with reduced-motion preferences, everything
  arrives already-composed — the page must be *complete* without motion,
  because motion here is tone of voice, not information.

## 10. Premium UI references

Study these for *specific* lessons, not general vibes:

- **apple.com (product pages)** — one idea per viewport; typography as
  hero; photography color-matched to the page background so image and UI
  feel like one surface.
- **linear.app** — hairline discipline; restraint in color; how "fast and
  precise" is communicated through spacing and 1px rules rather than
  words.
- **porsche.com (model pages)** — desire built through engineering
  detail; the spec-sheet as an aesthetic object; dark "gravitas" sections
  used sparingly.
- **aman.com** — the arrival feeling; enormous imagery with tiny, perfect
  serif type; navigation that whispers; booking (their "CTA") present but
  never shouting.
- **stripe.com** — typographic scale management; making dense credibility
  (docs, logos, numbers) feel airy.
- **lamborghini/rimac configurators — as anti-reference** — what
  aggression looks like, so we can do the opposite with the same
  precision.
- **The Ritz-Carlton / Cheval Blanc digital** — luxury hospitality
  handling multi-language, multi-market elegance (note how French/Arabic
  editions retain art direction).

## 11. Luxury inspiration (beyond screens)

- **Aman & Cheval Blanc arrival rituals** — the homepage's first viewport
  is an *arrival*, not a pitch: space, light, one line of welcome, one
  door forward.
- **Porsche Exclusive Manufaktur brochures** — craft photographed as
  engineering; captions as specifications; gold used as thread-stitch
  accent.
- **Hermès window displays** — one object, one idea, theatrical restraint;
  the discipline of leaving things out.
- **Tadao Ando / John Pawson architecture** — light as material; the arch
  and the wall; why one shadow can do the work of ten graphics.
- **Riyadh's Diriyah & Abu Dhabi's Louvre** — contemporary Gulf luxury:
  geometry, sand-warm palettes, modernity rooted in heritage — the
  register our Arabic-market audience recognizes as *theirs*, not as
  orientalist pastiche.
- **Swiss private clinics (La Prairie register)** — how medical authority
  and indulgence coexist: credentials typeset like a menu, science
  photographed like skincare.

## 12. The homepage emotional journey

Not a wireframe — a narrative arc for whoever designs the sections later:

1. **Arrival (0–3 seconds):** porcelain light, one serif line, one gold
   door. The visitor exhales. *"This is different."* — Trust.
2. **Recognition:** the place and the people — architecture and faces
   that look like the visitor's world across four markets. *"This is for
   people like me."* — Belonging.
3. **Evidence:** craft macro + quiet numbers + credentials set like a
   spec sheet. *"These people are serious."* — Confidence.
4. **Desire:** the outcome — genuine smiles, warm light, life lived.
   Never before/after grids; always *after*, beautifully. — Want.
5. **Invitation (ink & gold):** the dark, resonant close: one line, one
   golden action, whitespace like a held breath. *"Begin."* — Action.

Trust is built in registers 1–3; desire in 4; the conversion moment in 5
inherits both.

---

## 13. Summary — the ten commandments

1. Porcelain space, gold signature, one warm light.
2. Whitespace is the luxury; remove before you add.
3. One protagonist per viewport.
4. Gold is ~1% of the page, or it means nothing.
5. No blue, no pure white, no medical clichés.
6. The serif speaks; the sans works; Arabic is designed, never swapped.
7. Photography: Aman places, editorial people, Apple-grade craft macro.
8. Icons are hairline furniture, not decoration.
9. Narrative motion is slow and certain; interactive motion is quick and
   physical; stillness is always complete.
10. Every decision must make a discerning person feel *safer and more
    drawn in at once*. If it only does one, redo it.
